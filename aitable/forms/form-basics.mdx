## What is a form?

You've surely filled out forms before, such as registration forms, sign-up sheets, or surveys. In AI Table, a **form** is exactly that — an online questionnaire that captures structured info and data efficiently. With forms, you can design structured data input methods, generate and share a **link** or **QR code**, and allow others to fill out and submit information directly upon opening.

Every submission is automatically aggregated into your AI Table, seamlessly integrating with features like filtering, grouping, and dashboards. This provides you with powerful capabilities for data collection, organization, and analysis.

## **Example Use Cases**

- **Event Signup**: Create a form to collect participant details such as name, contact information, and attendance time. The collected data is automatically aggregated, allowing you to quickly filter for participants who meet specific criteria.
- **Feedback Collection**: Businesses can use forms to design customer satisfaction surveys and gather suggestions or opinions. Then leverage AI Tables’ grouping and statistical functions to categorize and quantitatively analyze the feedback.
- **Inventory Management**: Retail or manufacturing businesses can use forms to record product inbound and outbound data. By combining filtering and dashboard features, you can generate inventory status reports to help optimize inventory management.
- **Inspection Management**: Store managers can use forms to record inspection details, such as shelf displays, hygiene conditions, and out-of-stock items. Aggregated data in AI Tables can be grouped by store, and dashboards can generate inspection reports, enabling regional managers to quickly grasp overall operational status.
- **Employee leave Application**: Companies can use forms for employees to submit leave requests, including leave type, dates, and duration. This data is aggregated in AI Tables, making it easier for HR departments to review and compile statistics.
- **Student information Collection**: schools collect signups, parent contacts, after-school course choices. Teachers can quickly view statistics for each class through grouping and filtering, and intuitively display results using statistical charts.

As demonstrated by these scenarios, forms not only simplify the data collection process but also significantly improve the efficiency of data organization and analysis, making them ideal for any workflow that requires frequent information entry and processing.

## Editing a form

The editor has three areas:

**❶ Toolbar**

Switch to **Fill out**, view stats, pick a theme, change settings, publish, share.

**❷ Question picker**

- Available questions: existing fields / columns in the table.
- New questions: question types you can add (each maps to a field type in the table).

**❸ Form canvas**

Reorder, duplicate, hide, set defaults, mark required.

### Configure questions (fields)

A table's fields / columns and the form's questions are linked. **Each form question maps to one field / column in the table.** Edit or delete a question and the field / column updates accordingly; same the other way.

#### Add a question

Add via **Available questions** or **New questions**.

**Available questions**: fields that already exist in the table but aren't yet on the form — listed at the top-left of the canvas. Click the **+** next to a field, or drag it onto the canvas, to add it as a question. Use **Add all** or **Hide all** for bulk operations.

**New questions**: every question type the form supports, listed on the left. Each maps 1:1 to a field type. Click to append at the bottom, or drag to drop at a specific position.

#### Question description

Below each question, you can add a line of help text — explain what to fill in or what to watch for. It helps respondents answer accurately.

![](https://img.alicdn.com/imgextra/i2/O1CN01g55Vrh1Teus1xyLFh_!!6000000002408-2-tps-960-420.png)


Note: question description shows only on the form, for respondents. Field description in the table is for table readers — different audiences, different purposes.

#### Change question (field) type

Click a question — the field type shows at the top-left of the question card (e.g. Single select). Click the field type and the configuration popup opens. See **Working with fields** for details.

![](https://img.alicdn.com/imgextra/i4/O1CN01lwPImN1l6FyvbvimU_!!6000000004769-2-tps-694-412.png)


#### Reorder questions

Drag the question card to reorder.

Note: reordering on the form does not change field order in the table.

#### Hide / delete / duplicate (questions / fields)

Click a question — at the top-right, hide or duplicate it; in the **⋯** menu, delete it.

- **Hide**: form-only — the question disappears from the form (back to **Available questions**), but the field stays in the table.
- **Delete**: removes the field from the table — the data goes with it.
- **Duplicate**: copies the question, plus copies the field and its values in the table.

Note: the primary field (column 1 of the table) can't be deleted, but it can be hidden.

#### Mark required

Click a question — toggle **Required** at the top-right of the card.

![](https://img.alicdn.com/imgextra/i2/O1CN01Wpbeb21gzblkJPRjP_!!6000000004213-2-tps-1608-402.png)


#### Set a default value

Sometimes you want a question pre-filled when a user opens the form. For example: in an expense form, default **Need invoice?** to **No**.

Click a question — set the default at the top-right of the card.

![](https://img.alicdn.com/imgextra/i1/O1CN01CxjkY91OovRnU2MVt_!!6000000001753-2-tps-1594-446.png)


#### Conditional visibility

Sometimes a question should only show under certain conditions. For example: in a leave-request form, when an employee picks **Sick leave**, then show **Hospital** and **Sick leave certificate upload**.

Path: click the question, in the **⋯** menu pick **Conditional visibility**, and add the conditions.

![](https://img.alicdn.com/imgextra/i1/O1CN01NmxCMy1b72V70XlSZ_!!6000000003417-2-tps-1676-250.png)


## Form settings

Click **Settings** in the top toolbar to change form-level options.

**Data source**: read-only — only shown when editing a form on the form-collection page.

---
**Share form**: turn publishing on or off. Off = collection stops; even people with the link can't fill it in.

---
**Anonymous**: when on, respondent identity is not collected.

**Who can fill out**: Anyone in the org / Anyone / Only specified people. Add **Required respondents** — people, groups, or departments. With required respondents set, you can use **Remind** (DING / work notification / chat).

**No-login fill**: when on, anyone with the link can fill out the form (e.g. directly in WeChat). Submitter identity isn't collected.

---
**Limit per user**: limit each user to one submission, or one per day.

**Cap total submissions**: set a max — once hit, the form stops accepting submissions.

**Effective time**: respondents can only submit between the start and end times you set.

**Recurring reminder**: respondents get periodic reminders. For daily reports / weekly reports / sales updates / other recurring use cases. Captured data can be split per cycle — see [Form per-cycle splits](/aitable/forms/form-rotation).

---
**Allow respondents to edit submissions**: when on, respondents can edit their previous submission and re-submit; the new version overwrites.

**After submit**: redirect to an end page (with a custom message), or to a specified URL.

---
**Set defaults via share link**: see [Pre-fill form defaults](/aitable/forms/form-prefill).

## FAQ

#### Q: What's the relationship between a form and a table?

A: A form is the entry point for collection. Each question maps to a field / column in the table. Submissions auto-flow into the table.

#### Q: Who can edit a form?

A: With Advanced Permissions off — anyone with **Edit** or above. With Advanced Permissions on — only users with **Manage** on the form's table, plus Owner / Admin.

#### Q: **Do respondents have to log into DingTalk to fill out the form?**

A: No, not always. Turn on **No-login fill** in form settings — anyone can open the link in WeChat etc. and submit. The form collector can't see submitter identity in this case — they show up as **Anonymous**.

#### Q: Can I edit a form after publishing?

A: Yes. Edits to questions / options sync to table fields. Be careful with deletes — they erase data.

#### Q: How do I pause / stop collection?

A: Toggle the share switch off — collection stops; people opening the link see "collection ended." Toggle back on to resume; the link doesn't change.

#### Q: How do I see who submitted?

A: Add a **Created by** field to the response table. Add **Created at** for submission time too. These don't show on the form to respondents. With no-login fill or anonymous on, they show as **Anonymous**.

#### Q: Is the data secure?

A: All form-collected data is stored securely in AI Table. Only users with the right permissions can view or manage it.

#### Q: Is there a cap on submissions?

A: Yes — if the form admin set **Cap total submissions**, once hit the form stops accepting. And since submissions sync to the table, the table's row cap also applies.

#### Q: **Can people outside the org fill out the form?**

A: Yes. On the share page, set the audience to **Anyone**. You can invite external users. If you've added member / group / department questions, the audience can't be set to **Anyone** — that's a data-safety guard.

#### Q: **For per-period limits, what timezone does the period use?**

A: The doc's timezone.
