# Third-Party Business Approval Category Integration: Custom Business Groups in the To-Do Center

## Overview

Many organizations run multiple business systems, so approval tasks arrive from mixed sources. Managers want to filter third-party approvals by business in the DingTalk To-Do Center. This solution uses the custom approval business group API from the DingTalk OA Approval Process Center. Third-party business systems sync their internal business groups to the DingTalk Approval Center. DingTalk then creates matching second-level business source groups under the **OA Approval** category of the To-Do Center. Approvers can filter third-party To-Dos by group. Managers who handle many approvals review them faster.

### Background

As an organization grows, approval types multiply: leave requests, expense reimbursements, purchases, contracts, seal usage, and more. By default, the DingTalk To-Do Center lists all To-Dos in chronological order. Employees struggle to find high-priority approvals or approvals of a specific business type. Custom approval business groups categorize approvals by business domain, urgency, source system, and other dimensions. This improves processing efficiency.

Organizations commonly face the following pain points:

- **Cluttered Task list**: Dozens of approval types appear together. Employees must identify the business type of each item one by one, and important approvals get buried among routine ones.
- **Hard to distinguish cross-system approvals**: Approvals from OA, expense control, HR, and other systems look the same. Employees cannot easily identify the approval source or processing urgency.
- **Inefficient batch processing**: Similar approvals, such as batches of expense reports, cannot be filtered by group. Employees must open and process each one, and repetitive actions account for over 60% of the work.
- **No management view**: Department heads cannot view their team's To-Do distribution by business type. Staffing and resource allocation decisions lack data support.

### Core Value

This solution delivers value in three areas:

- **Clear business groups:** DingTalk automatically creates second-level business groups for third-party systems under the **OA Approval** category of the To-Do Center. Approvers can quickly filter target approval tasks by group.
- **Automatic category sync:** Third-party systems call the API to sync business groups to DingTalk. New and modified groups take effect in real time, with no manual maintenance of categories on the DingTalk side.
- **Flexible extension:** Third-party systems can define custom group names, multilingual labels, and descriptions, and configure multi-level group structures to match their business architecture.

### Use Cases

This solution suits organizations whose approval tasks come from diverse sources and whose business lines are clearly divided. Typical use cases include:

- **Multi-business-line approval aggregation:** A group company has several independent business units, each using a different approval system. Managers want to locate To-Dos by business line in DingTalk.
- **Frequent cross-department approvals:** Daily approvals involve procurement, HR, finance, and other functions. Approvers frequently switch filters to find specific approval types.
- **Refined To-Do Center operations:** The organization wants to improve the DingTalk To-Do Center experience. Custom business groups help approvers find target tasks faster and reduce time spent browsing.

## Typical Business Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Multi-Business Approval Categorization for a Smart Agricultural Park (Agritech Industry)

#### Pain Points

A large smart agricultural park runs four business segments: crop farming, livestock farming, agricultural product processing, and agricultural supply sales. Each segment processes approvals in its own system: an in-house crop management system, a livestock IoT platform, an ERP processing system, and an e-commerce back office. The four systems run approval tasks separately. The park's general manager and segment heads handle many approvals every day, and the existing process has the following problems:

- **Mixed business lines are hard to filter:** The park handles 80+ approvals per day. All approvals from the four systems flow into the DingTalk To-Do Center with no business categorization. Finding an approval for "crop irrigation equipment purchase" means scrolling through 80+ mixed tasks, which takes 3 minutes on average.
- **Slow response to cross-segment approvals:** The general manager tracks approval progress across all four segments. Traditionally this requires signing in to four separate systems, which carries a high risk of oversight. Delayed responses to urgent matters, such as repairing a temperature control failure in the livestock area, put production safety at risk.
- **Poor To-Do Center experience:** After receiving an OA Approval message card, approvers open the To-Do Center and see a flat list with no way to filter by business line. New managers need up to 2 weeks to adapt.

#### Value Validation

- **Automated Flow**

  The automated integration flow consists of five steps:

  1. **Multi-system access**: Connect multiple business systems (for example, four business lines) to DingTalk.
  2. **Aggregated To-Do display**: Show all pending items in a unified view with source-based filtering.
  3. **Business group creation**: Obtain the **dirId** identifier for each group.
  4. **Template-to-group binding**: Bind an approval template to a group through **AFFILIATION_DIR**.
  5. **Closed-loop status sync**: Keep data consistent through callback events.
- **Key Advantages**

  - **Clear groups:** Approvals from the four business lines are categorized automatically. Finding a target approval drops from 3 minutes to 5 seconds, saving 20 minutes of filtering per day.
  - **Real-time sync:** New business groups, such as a "Smart Greenhouse" subgroup, take effect as soon as the API is called. No manual maintenance of DingTalk categories is required.
  - **Consistent across devices:** Custom groups appear in the To-Do Center on both PC and mobile, so approvers can filter by business line on the go.

### Scenario 2: Multi-Channel Content Approval Integration for a Media Group (Culture and Media Industry)

#### Pain Points

A provincial culture and media group operates four content channels: news, entertainment, sports, and documentaries. It uses an in-house CMS for manuscript approvals, a copyright management system for licensing approvals, an advertising system for ad placement approvals, and a finance system for budget approvals. The four systems run their approval workflows independently. The chief editor's office director and channel directors handle many approvals every day, and the existing process has the following problems:

- **Mixed content approvals:** The group handles 100+ approvals per day. News articles, entertainment scripts, sports broadcast rights, ad placement contracts, and other approvals all appear together in the DingTalk To-Do Center. The chief editor's office director needs to review news articles quickly, but they get buried among other approval types, hurting the timeliness of important stories.
- **Copyright approvals are easy to miss:** Copyright licensing approvals are highly time-sensitive and become invalid once expired. In a flat list, other approvals easily obscure them. Three incidents occurred where expired, unrenewed copyrights forced programs to be taken down.
- **No view for channel directors:** Each channel director cares only about their own channel's approvals, but the To-Do Center has no channel filter. Directors must manually identify the owner of each approval and waste 30 minutes a day browsing.

#### Value Validation

- **Automated Flow**

  The automated integration flow consists of five stages:

  1. **Multi-system access**: Connect the CMS, copyright, advertising, and finance systems to the content hub.
  2. **To-Do aggregation**: View pending items in a unified **To-Do Center**, filtered by source.
  3. **Business group creation**: Obtain the `dirId` identifier for each group.
  4. **Template-to-group linking**: Bind the approval template to its group through `AFFILIATION_DIR`.
  5. **Closed-loop status sync**: Callback events keep data consistent across all systems.

  Each OA Approval card shows the following fields: **From** (content hub), **Channel**, **Template**, and **Group ID** (for example, `DIR_1001`), with **Approve** and **Reject** actions.
- **Key Advantages**

  - **Precise filtering:** Approvals from the four channels are categorized automatically. The chief editor's office director finds news article approvals in 5 seconds instead of 3 minutes, with zero delays on important stories.
  - **Copyright protection:** Copyright licensing approvals go into a dedicated group and stand out, so none are missed. Copyright expiration incidents drop to zero.
  - **Role-based views:** Each channel director sees only their own channel's group. Wasted browsing time drops from 30 minutes to zero, and focus improves significantly.

## Implementation Guide

### Technical Architecture

The technical architecture of this solution includes the following core components:

- **Authentication layer:** Manages App credentials and obtains access tokens to verify the identity behind every API call.
- **Group sync layer:** Calls the API to create or update a business group, syncs business groups from the third-party system to the DingTalk Approval Center, and obtains the unique group code `dirId`.
- **Template association layer:** Calls the API to save an externally integrated approval template in Process Center. Uses the `AFFILIATION_DIR` field in `processFeatureConfig` to reference the `dirId` returned earlier and bind the approval template to the business group.
- **Task creation layer:** Calls the API to create or update approval To-Do tasks, syncs approval tasks from the third-party system to the DingTalk Approval Center, and assigns them to the corresponding business group automatically.
- **Event callback layer:** Listens for approval task status change callback events to keep data consistent between the third-party system and the DingTalk Approval Center.

### Prerequisites

- **App preparation:** Complete the [app creation and configuration](/open/dingstart/create-application) process.
- **Permission requirements:** Hold DingTalk Organization Admin or Sub Admin permissions, and apply for the following API permissions:

  - `qyapi_aflow` (approval workflow data management permission)
  - `Workflow.Form.Write` (workflow template write permission)
  - `Workflow.Form.Read` (workflow instance write permission)
  - `Premium.Workflow.ReadWrite.All` (OA Approval workflow read/write permission)
- **Development environment:** Install a Java development environment (JDK 1.6 or later) and the Maven build tool.
- **SDK preparation:** Download the DingTalk Server-side SDK, which supports Java, Python, Go, and other languages.

### Code Implementation

Step 1: Go to the app details page and get the client ID and Client Secret of the app.

Step 2: Apply for API permissions. Apply for the relevant "OA Approval" permissions.

Step 3: Get the App Access credential. See [Get the access token of an Internal app](/open/development/obtain-the-access-token-of-an-internal-app). When calling APIs, use the access token to authenticate the caller.

Step 4: Call the OA Approval APIs:

1. Call the new Server API to create or update a business group and obtain the group `dirId`.
2. Call the new Server API to save an externally integrated approval template in Process Center and obtain the unique template code `processCode`.
3. If you did not save the `processCode`, call the [Get template code](/open/development/obtain-the-template-code) API to obtain it.
4. After the approval template is created, users can go to the DingTalk OA Approval Admin Console to view third-party approval form templates, view and search template data, and export or delete template data.
5. Use the template code `processCode` to call the new Server API to save an externally integrated approval instance in Process Center. This initiates an approval instance and returns the approval instance ID `processInstanceId`.
6. After the instance is created, users can go to the DingTalk OA Approval Center to view the four approval lists (Pending, Processed, Initiated, and Received), search approval instance data, and process approvals.
7. Use `processInstanceId` and `tasks` to call the API to save externally integrated approval tasks in Process Center. This returns the taskId of each To-Do and creates the corresponding DingTalk To-Do task.
8. After the pending task is created, call the [Query OA approval tasks integrated through Process Center](/open/development/query-oa-approval-tasks-integrated-through-process-center) API to query the user's running approval tasks.
9. Use `processInstanceId` and `taskId` to call the [Update Process Center task status](/open/development/update-process-center-task-status) API to sync status updates for your own approval To-Dos. In scenarios such as OR approval (where any one approver can decide), call the [Cancel Process Center pending tasks in batches](/open/development/cancel-multiple-oa-approval-tasks) API to set all running To-Dos under an approval instance to CANCELED.
10. Update the instance status based on the approval instance `processInstanceId`, `status`, the instance result, and other data, using approval task events (start, complete, transfer) or approval instance events (start, complete, terminate, delete).
11. To delete a business group, call the API to delete a business group. This deletes the group synced from the third-party system to DingTalk OA Approval, along with the corresponding second-level category under the OA Approval category in DingTalk To-Do.
12. To clean up approval template data, call the [Delete template](/open/development/self-owned-approval-deletion-template) API. This deletes the approval template created for the organization, along with the instances and To-Do tasks created under it.

## Implementation Steps

### **Step 1: Get the App Credential**

1. Sign in to the [DingTalk Developer Console](https://open-dev.dingtalk.io/).
2. Select the target app to open its details page.
3. Click **Basic Information** > **Credentials and Basic Information**.
4. Record the app's `Client ID` and `Client Secret`.

### Note

   Keep the `Client Secret` secure and never share it with third parties. Store it in environment variables or a key management system instead of hardcoding it.

### Step 2: Add API Permissions

1. On the app details page, click **Manage permissions**.
2. Enter the following permission identifiers in the permission search box and apply for them:

   - `qyapi_aflow` (approval workflow data management permission): Used to query and update Process Center task status.
   - `Workflow.Form.Write` (workflow template write permission): Used to get the template code.
   - `Workflow.Form.Read` (workflow template read permission): Used to create or update approval instances and delete templates.
   - `Premium.Workflow.ReadWrite.All` (OA Approval workflow read/write permission): Used to create or update business groups.

<Note>
Different business scenarios may require different permission combinations. Apply for permissions based on your actual needs.
</Note>

### Step 3: Get the Access Credential (Access Token)

Use the Client ID and Client Secret from Step 1 to get the App Access credential. See [Get the access token of an Internal app](/open/development/obtain-the-access-token-of-an-internal-app).

**Best practices**:

- **Caching strategy**: The `accessToken` is valid for 2 hours. Cache it in memory or Redis, and refresh it proactively 5 minutes before it expires.
- **Concurrency control**: Prevent multiple threads from refreshing the token at the same time and causing conflicts. Use a distributed lock.
- **Error retry**: Retry automatically on network fluctuations, up to 3 times, with increasing intervals (1s → 2s → 4s).

### Step 4: Call the Core APIs

1. **Sync approval business groups to DingTalk**: Call the new Server API to create or update a business group and obtain the group `dirId`. This syncs business groups from the third-party system to DingTalk OA Approval and creates the corresponding second-level categories under the OA Approval category in DingTalk To-Do.

### Note

   When you later sync pending tasks to DingTalk, you can specify the business group each To-Do task belongs to. This helps approvers review To-Dos from third-party business systems more clearly.

   ```text
   Request example:
   {
     "operateUserId":"manager9814",
     "bizGroup":"administeration",
     "name": "Test",
     "name18n": "{\"en_US\":\"test\",\"ja_JP\":\"test\",\"vi_VN\":\"test\",\"zh_CN\":\"测试\",\"zh_HK\":\"测试\",\"zh_TW\":\"测试\"}",
     "description": "Custom approval business group test"
   }

   Response example:
   {
     "result": {
       "bizGroup": "348925476_administeration20250210",
       "dirId": "194e436699fe71353fc1f3d498f80e66"
     },
     "success": true
   }
   ```

### Request Parameters

   - `operateUserId`: The user ID of the operator.
   - `bizGroup`: The business group identifier, used to distinguish groups.
   - `name`: The Chinese name of the group.
   - `description`: The group description.

### Response Fields

   - `bizGroup`: The full business group identifier.
   - `dirId`: The unique group code, used later to associate templates.
2. **Sync approval template data to DingTalk**: Call the new Server API to save an externally integrated approval template in Process Center and obtain the unique template code `processCode`. **In this scenario, use AFFILIATION_DIR in processFeatureConfig (the Process Center integration configuration) to specify the dirId group returned in step 1.**

## FAQ

- **Q: How many business groups can a third-party system create?**

  Each app supports up to 10 business groups. Plan groups based on your actual number of business lines, and avoid over-segmenting, which can confuse approvers.
- **Q: Can I rename a business group after creating it?**

  Yes. Call the API to create or update a business group again, passing the same `bizGroup` identifier and the new `name` to update the group name.
- **Q: What happens to associated approval tasks after a business group is deleted?**

  Deleting a business group does not delete existing approval tasks or templates. However, these tasks lose their group assignment and appear as ungrouped in the To-Do Center. Before deleting a group, confirm that no active tasks are associated with it.
