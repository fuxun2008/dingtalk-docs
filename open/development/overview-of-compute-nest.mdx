## What Is Compute Nest

Compute Nest is a service management platform that Alibaba Cloud provides to enterprise application service providers. On Compute Nest, service providers manage their apps within a unified environment, run one-click cloud resource and application diagnostics, and use an integrated monitoring, event collection, and alerting system to keep applications running stably.

## Option 1: Alibaba Cloud + Compute Nest + Contacts Encryption

### Core Process

### Process Overview

1. When onboarding a product solution provider, the organization has already signed the [Shared Security Responsibility and Security Commitment](https://partner.dingtalk.com/indexReource#/isv_service/contract_manage) on the Partner Platform.
2. Sign in to the [DingTalk Developer Console](https://open-dev.dingtalk.com/?spm=dd_developers.header.unLogin.openDevBtn#/) to associate an Alibaba Cloud account, and authorize DingTalk to perform security risk checks and vulnerability scans. For details, see App Deployment.
3. In the associated Alibaba Cloud account, create a dedicated VPC to deploy the DingTalk app for network isolation, and register the app resources with Compute Nest.
4. The published app you develop has integrated Contacts Encryption.
5. The published app you develop has applied for and configured access to a [DingTalk Security Domain](/zh/open/development/config-domain-name).
6. The published app you develop has passed the [Application Self-Check](/zh/open/development/application-self-check) process, and has submitted the security admission review materials and passed the review.

### Other Deployment Security Requirements

1. The database must not be configured with a public network address to connect directly to external systems or export data externally.
2. Personal sensitive information (such as phone numbers, ID card numbers, bank account numbers, and passwords) must be classified, encrypted, and de-identified (for example, masked) in accordance with the requirements of the Personal Information Protection Law.
3. Follow the security requirements agreed upon with DingTalk, and take responsibility for cooperating with security checks organized by DingTalk.

## Reference: Determining Personal Sensitive Information

-- Source: GB/T 35273—2020 "Information Security Technology - Personal Information Security Specification"

Personal sensitive information refers to personal information that, once leaked, illegally provided, or misused, may endanger personal and property safety and easily lead to damage to personal reputation, physical and mental health, or discriminatory treatment. In general, the personal information of children aged 14 and under and information involving the privacy of natural persons constitute personal sensitive information. You can determine whether information is personal sensitive information from the following perspectives:

Leakage: Once personal information is leaked, the personal information subject and the organizations and institutions that collect and use the personal information lose control over it, making the scope of dissemination and the purpose of use uncontrollable. After some personal information is leaked, it may be used directly against the will of the personal information subject or subjected to correlation analysis with other information, potentially posing significant risks to the rights and interests of the personal information subject, and should therefore be determined as personal sensitive information. For example, a copy of the personal information subject's ID card is used by others for real-name registration of phone SIM cards or opening bank accounts and cards.

Illegal provision: Some personal information poses significant risks to the rights and interests of the personal information subject merely by being disseminated beyond the scope authorized and consented to by the subject, and should be determined as personal sensitive information. Examples include sexual orientation, deposit information, and infectious disease history.

Misuse: Some personal information, when used beyond the reasonable limits of authorization (such as changing the processing purpose or expanding the processing scope), may pose significant risks to the rights and interests of the personal information subject, and should be determined as personal sensitive information. For example, using health information for insurance company marketing and to determine individual premium levels without the authorization of the personal information subject.

Examples of personal sensitive information:

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Personal property information | Bank accounts, authentication information (passwords), deposit information (including amount of funds, payment and receipt records, etc.), real estate information, credit records, credit reference information, transaction and consumption records, transaction flow records, etc., as well as virtual property information such as virtual currency, virtual transactions, and game redemption codes |
| Personal health and physiological information | Records generated when an individual is treated for illness, such as symptoms, hospitalization records, medical orders, test reports, surgical and anesthesia records, nursing records, medication records, drug and food allergy information, reproductive information, past medical history, diagnosis and treatment, family medical history, current medical history, infectious disease history, etc. |
| Personal biometric information | Personal genes, fingerprints, voiceprints, palmprints, auricles, irises, facial recognition features, etc. |
| Personal identity information | ID cards, military officer cards, passports, driver's licenses, work permits, social security cards, residence permits, etc. |
| Other information | Sexual orientation, marriage history, religious beliefs, undisclosed criminal records, communication records and content, contacts, contacts lists, group lists, travel trajectories, web browsing history, accommodation information, precise location information, etc. |
