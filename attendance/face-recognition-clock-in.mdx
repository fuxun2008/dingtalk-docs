DingTalk Attendance offers multiple verification methods when employees check in, verifying whether the person checks in themselves, preventing proxy check-ins, and helping organizations build a fair and impartial work environment. The following describes three common verification methods that prevent proxy check-ins.

## Three anti-proxy check-in verification methods

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check-in phone recognition">
    Employees can only check in with their own phones. When an employee uses their own phone to check in on behalf of someone else, the action is recorded and triggers a security check. Admins can review and confirm this in the attendance records.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Photo check-in">
    When employees check in (especially when visiting clients off-site), they can upload a photo taken with the client to confirm that they have arrived at the work location and started working. Admins can review the photo for confirmation in the attendance records.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Facial recognition">
    When employees check in, facial recognition can quickly verify whether the person checks in themselves. If someone checks in on behalf of another person, they cannot pass facial recognition.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
DingTalk Attendance continues to iterate on the product to strengthen compliance. In line with the requirements for protecting facial sensitive personal information under the Personal Information Protection Law and other relevant laws and regulations, the DingTalk platform iterates the product promptly. For organizations that enable facial attendance check-in scenarios, it upgrades the facial base image enrollment to require separate individual authorization, and clarifies the scenarios and scope of data use. For existing facial photos (including facial check-ins triggered by attendance errors), it optimizes data retention and applies deletion, protecting personal sensitive information in accordance with the law.
</Info>
